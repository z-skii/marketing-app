Reviewer: Astra, TapMart's design director.

Captures: user-home-m.png, user-home-s.png, user-home-t.png, user-home-u.png, user-home-d.png, user-home-l.png, user-home-m-preview.png, user-home-d-preview.png, home-open-m-strip.png

**Verdict: fix.** On phone: recreate this reference for US$75 per approved version, then View. Desktop immediately adds US$25 for a 24-hour post after approval and US$300 per month for a vehicle campaign. These are opportunities, not an earned balance.

## The ten questions

- Does this feel like a premium modern product? **partly**. The composition, palette and money hierarchy do. The 390px Search label and cropped compact reference remain visible finishing defects.
- Does it explain itself without paragraphs? **yes**. Activity, amount, payment basis and View are sufficient. The phone first screen has 34 words instead of production's 90.
- Does it feel like a consumer platform, not business software? **yes**. Images and opportunities lead. There is no balance dashboard, administrative introduction or collection of status panels.
- Is it memorable? **yes**. The unequal portrait, narrow creative and landscape vehicle meeting the same straight edge create a recognizable TapMart composition.
- Does motion improve understanding? **partly**. The strip preserves the reference and US$75 through opening, exposes destination content immediately and shows focus returning to the source. Intermediate movement and reduced-motion behavior are not sufficiently evidenced.
- Does each earning type feel different? **yes**. Desktop clearly distinguishes making a version, posting finished creative and driving with a campaign through media shape, verbs and payment conditions. The complete phone Story and Car compositions are not shown.
- Is business discovery exciting? **partly**. Business Home is not pictured. This User Home is inviting, but it cannot establish the quality of people discovery or requesting work.
- Is Profile identity, not settings? **partly**. Profile is only a navigation destination here. Its identity presentation cannot be judged from these captures.
- Does the website make someone keep scrolling? **partly**. No public website is shown. The next Story title provides continuation on User Home, but that is not evidence for the website.
- Is it significantly stronger than current production? **yes**. The work is much larger, payment conditions are easier to find, competing controls are reduced and desktop finally has an intentional composition.

## Spec drift

- Reference containment is not implemented as specified. The 320px image fills its nearly square capped frame rather than fitting wholly inside it; the larger feed frames also crop the taller original visible in the preview. Preserve the stages but contain the source.
- The 390px header displays Search text, although phone utilities were specified as icons with accessible names. Remove that visible label without removing the control or reducing its target.

## Spec was wrong

- Putting every task title below its media was too rigid. Keep Recreate this Reel above the reference at 320px, and keep the phone Story title before its composition. These changes establish the activity before someone scrolls through the image.
- My filled 4:5 reference dimensions assumed a compatible source. The opened reference in the motion strip reveals a taller original. Preserve the allocated stage and first-screen money access, but let the complete image occupy a narrower, centered area rather than forcing it to fill.
- The original Car discovery band left approval implicit. Keep the added Approval required beside the monthly rate; /month describes frequency, not entitlement to payment.

## Keep

- The desktop composition: unequal media silhouettes meeting one continuous earning edge. Preserve the empty paper above Car rather than filling it with another panel.
- The warm paper, green-black typography, restrained brick terminals, square media and flat surfaces. No card containers, shadows or decorative motion.
- The large, unambiguous amounts, visible payment conditions and one View action per opportunity. Keep Vehicle example and Approval required.
- The four labelled Personal destinations, aligned desktop labels, Activity count placement and tablet utilities appearing only in the header.
- The complete Story artwork in both previews, visible Close label, readable requirements and explicit separation between approval, earnings and payout.
- The current text discipline. All measured default feeds meet the budget, and both phone first screens remain below 40 words.

## Why better than production

Production makes people work through navigation, instructions and small thumbnails to understand an opportunity. This version puts the work, its payment conditions and one View action together. Desktop presents three clearly different ways to earn in one deliberate composition; phone gives the leading opportunity room to be understood. Default copy falls from 199 to 57 words on phone and 203 to 60 on desktop. The familiar four destinations remain. This is a substantial improvement in clarity and character, with two rendering defects still preventing final approval.

## Remaining risks

- The full phone Story rail and Car opportunity are below these resting captures. Supply a full-feed phone capture after the fixes, plus the 320px safe-area and 200% text-zoom checks. The supplied measurements establish no horizontal overflow at the six baseline widths.
- The strip establishes opening and return states, not precise animation timing, reduced motion, browser Back behavior or scroll restoration. These still require interaction verification.
- Expanded Money details and Terms, disabled commitment actions, and alternate eligibility, missing-data and error states are not fully evidenced here. This review does not authorize production mutations.
- Before and after use different records and five versus three rendered images. The structural improvement is clear, but the density reduction is not a record-for-record content comparison.
- Business Home, Profile and the public website require their own reviews. Their associated 5/10 scores are neutral unassessed markers, not judgments of those screens.
