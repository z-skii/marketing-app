## The ten questions

- Does this feel like a premium modern product? **partly**. Phone and large desktop are polished and deliberately composed. The upper-tablet media gap and 768px money wrapping still look unfinished.
- Does it explain itself without paragraphs? **yes**. Faces, work, Request and the placement-specific monthly ask communicate the main choices. Measured phone first screens contain 24 words, with no default block of 18 or more words.
- Does it feel like a consumer platform, not business software? **yes**. People and photographs lead. Administration is absent, and operational attention stays in navigation rather than becoming a dashboard.
- Is it memorable? **yes**. The portrait/work montage meeting the straight earning edge is recognisable. It is more distinctive than a conventional creator-card grid.
- Does motion improve understanding? **partly**. The strip preserves the same person and shows source focus on return. It mostly contains settled states, so it does not establish the shared-bounds transition, its timing or reduced-motion behaviour.
- Does each earning type feel different? **partly**. Car advertising clearly differs from requesting a person. Story and Recreate are separate contextual choices, but their bare labels do not yet explain the different work to an unfamiliar business.
- Is business discovery exciting? **yes**. Large faces and inspectable work make choosing someone inviting. The car adds a genuinely different advertising possibility instead of another repetitive shelf.
- Is Profile identity, not settings? **partly**. The person preview is identity-and-work led, but it is not the Profile screen. Profile is unassessed; its score of 0 denotes missing evidence, not a failed design.
- Does the website make someone keep scrolling? **partly**. This marketplace offers a good next-object rhythm, but no public website capture is supplied. The website scrolling score of 0 denotes unassessed evidence.
- Is it significantly stronger than current production? **yes**. The before captures are thumbnail-heavy and repetitive. V2 makes people, work and the next action substantially easier to recognise while preserving useful navigation and financial qualifications.

## Why better than production

Production makes you read small thumbnails, repeated shelves and campaign reminders before deciding whom to approach. This version puts a person’s face, work and Request action together. Car advertising has a different shape, a prominent monthly price and an explicit placement-specific asking rate. Familiar navigation remains, while review counts stop interrupting discovery. Reported default text falls from 208 to 41 words on phone and 249 to 53 on desktop—roughly 80% less. This is a substantial structural improvement, not just a warmer palette. It is close, but the remaining responsive defects prevent final approval.
