**Verdict: fix.** Your everyday can earn: film something, post an ad or advertise on a car. There is a clear second entrance for businesses.

## The ten questions

- Does this feel like a premium modern product? **partly**. Typography, palette and media treatment are strong. Unbalanced desktop stages, colored reference gutters and the inline subscription summary still look unfinished.
- Does it explain itself without paragraphs? **yes**. The photographs, three verbs, amounts and compact approval sequence explain the proposition. Material financial qualifiers remain readable.
- Does it feel like a consumer platform, not business software? **yes**. People, creative and cars lead. There are no dashboard statistics, generic feature grids or administrative navigation on the public page.
- Is it memorable? **partly**. The staggered hero and straight earning edge establish an identity. Repeated coffee imagery and the same large car appearing twice consecutively weaken the sequence.
- Does motion improve understanding? **partly**. The strip establishes a transition from editorial imagery to labelled opportunities without simulated earnings. It does not show the Story interval or demonstrate the intended shared-edge transformation clearly enough to approve the choreography.
- Does each earning type feel different? **yes**. Recreate uses a reference still, Post has a narrow information rail beside finished creative, and Drive is landscape media with monthly money.
- Is business discovery exciting? **partly**. The opening portrait-and-work composition is inviting. The phone repeats the vehicle, while desktop Find cars and Review work leave too much inert space.
- Is Profile identity, not settings? **partly**. No Profile screen is supplied, so this cannot be approved here. The public person example is identity-led, but that is not evidence of Profile behavior.
- Does the website make someone keep scrolling? **partly**. The hero and tonal cut create curiosity. The 5,623px phone page still spends too much distance on repeated media and vertically stacked action bands.
- Is it significantly stronger than current production? **yes**. The proposition is larger, more visual and substantially less verbose than the supplied production opening. It needs refinement, not a return to production's miniature interface and explanatory copy.

## Why better than production

This is a substantial improvement, not just a palette change. Production asks visitors to read an explanation and decipher a miniature app screen. V2 shows filming, posting and car advertising through large media, then connects each activity to readable money and an action. The warm canvas, distinctive display type, square photography and dark business chapter feel more considered. Reported phone text falls from 995 to 187 words and page height from 11,790 to 5,623px. However, repeated vehicle imagery, loose desktop compositions and unfinished selection behavior still prevent approval.

## Remaining risks

- Scores use a 0–10 scale; Profile's middle score indicates insufficient evidence, not approval of an unseen screen.
- The source disclosure identifies generated fixture media. These existing labelled fixtures must remain isolated lab examples, not become claims about real creators, inventory or campaign results.
- The engineer cites settings defaults for US$99 and US$199. Confirm that these are the effective read-only configured prices and that the currency is authoritative; otherwise show Price unavailable. A source-code default alone does not establish current billing configuration.
- No payout capture is visible in this supplied set. The reported missing payout fee must remain Not available and must not read as a zero fee.
- The motion strip's omission of Post may be sampling rather than a runtime defect. A denser recording is required before diagnosing or approving the progression.
- Screenshots cannot establish keyboard semantics, focus trapping, Back behavior, non-mutation guarantees or reduced-motion equivalence.
- The reported text reductions are promising, but final acceptance requires an all-panel count and separate disclosure counts rather than only the default active desktop state.
- Do not achieve the page-height target by cropping obligations, shrinking type, removing unique examples or inventing replacement media.
