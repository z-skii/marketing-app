# TapMart V3 experience brief

Recorded 2026-09-17 from the founder's instruction after the Loyalty concept
and architecture were approved to keep. This is the brief for the whole V3
experience. The Loyalty lab is one part of it, not its completion.

## Standing rules

- Everything stays isolated under /design-lab-v3 and docs/design-lab-v3.
- No production migration, no production routes, no real Wallet
  integration, no live database, no notification delivery.
- Keep the Loyalty foundation already built: architecture, first touch
  attribution, campaign and creator source, member model, visits and
  points, reward unlock, redemption, QR flows, Wallet concepts, privacy
  model, aggregate creator attribution, event history, the Apple and Google
  Wallet research. Redesign its visual surfaces as necessary so they belong
  inside the final V3 language.
- Astra is the design director. Claude Code builds. A person approves.
- The final Astra pass happens after the final fixes. The package never
  ends with "fixes were applied after review".
- When complete, stop and wait for approval.

## The quality bar

The whole TapMart experience should reach the quality and feel that made
the founder point to Artec (see ARTEC_LIVE_STUDY.md for what was actually
retrieved from the live site):

- premium
- highly visual
- very little copy
- Apple quality glass and material
- meaningful animation
- product screens integrated into the experience
- the creator side feels desirable
- the business side feels valuable
- the website feels like an interactive product film
- not another dashboard redesign

## The five core surfaces

1. Public homepage (the priority)
2. User Home
3. User Profile
4. Business Home
5. Business Loyalty

## Public homepage

Not headline, paragraph, screenshot, repeated. The website behaves like a
product experience. The visitor quickly understands TapMart has two sides:
MAKE MONEY and GROW YOUR BUSINESS. The strongest visual way to present those
two worlds is open; the wording is open.

User story, shown not explained, each with an actual visual sequence and
very little copy:

- RECREATE: reference media, creator version, submit, approved, earnings.
- POST: Story creative, phone, post, proof, earnings.
- DRIVE: real car, placement, campaign, monthly earning.
- GET PAID.

Business story: FIND PEOPLE, FIND CARS, CREATE, REVIEW, CONTENT, LOYALTY,
then the full loop ATTENTION, CUSTOMER, RETURN CUSTOMER, where Loyalty
becomes powerful: creator Story, customer, Loyalty signup, Wallet card,
repeat visit, reward, business sees source attribution. Done visually, not
in five paragraphs.

Three required wow moments, product and motion together, not decoration:
one from user earning, one from the car, one from the business to Loyalty
loop. Astra invents the strongest executions. Illustrative only: a Reel
transforms from reference into creator submission and finishes as
earnings; a real vehicle moves through space and an ad placement attaches
to it; a campaign or customer interaction turns into a Wallet loyalty
card, then a repeat visit, then attribution back to the originating
creator.

## Material

The earlier Open Cut direction did not capture what the founder meant. A
much more refined material system: transparent depth, layered surfaces,
refraction, controlled blur, content visibly moving beneath material,
subtle highlights, physical hierarchy, restrained shadows, responsive
materials, excellent motion easing. Not backdrop blur plus border plus
rounded rectangle on everything. Glass feels like a real material and is
used selectively.

## User Home

The first question: WHAT CAN I EARN FROM RIGHT NOW. Reduce visible UI
aggressively. Opportunity discovery commonly needs only media, money, short
context, action; details after interaction. Recreate, Story and Car should
not use the same card; they should feel physically different.

## User Profile

Something a user would actually share, like tapmart.live/@username.
Identity, not account administration. Visible: portrait, name, reputation,
work, Instagram, vehicle, public achievements. Private earnings stay private
where appropriate. One settings gear handles administration.

## Business Home

The question: WHO OR WHAT CAN GROW MY BUSINESS. People and cars feel
desirable, not a directory, not CRM rows. Show work, creator personality,
vehicles beautifully. Selecting a creator or car feels like choosing
creative inventory, not opening a database record.

## Loyalty placement

Decide the cleanest permanent Business architecture for Loyalty. Do not
automatically add it to the bottom navigation. Show the reasoning. It should
be easy to discover and important, but not navigation clutter.

## Motion requirement

Static screenshots are not enough. Real recordings of actual coded
interactions: hero, audience switch, Recreate, Story, Car, Business
discovery, Loyalty loop, User Home opportunity to detail and return, creator
expand and request, vehicle interaction, Loyalty visit to reward.

## Not everything is a card

Before using a card, ask whether it needs to be one. Prefer media stages,
layers, sheets, full bleed objects, strips, floating material, inline
expansion and spatial arrangements where they are better.

## Text discipline

Run a visible word count. Targets, used as pressure rather than hard rules:
User Home first mobile viewport roughly 20 to 35 words; Profile first
mobile viewport roughly 20 to 30; Business Home first mobile viewport
roughly 20 to 35; homepage hero ideally under 20 meaningful words besides
navigation and actions. If a feature needs 80 words, the design has
probably failed.

## Astra's questions

Not merely "is this premium". Judge the experience:

1. Would someone keep scrolling?
2. Does motion reveal the product?
3. Is this more desirable than production?
4. Is the hierarchy instantly understood?
5. Does this feel like a consumer product?
6. Does the Business side justify paying?
7. Does Loyalty strengthen the story?
8. Are we actually at the quality benchmark?
9. Are there at least three memorable product moments?
